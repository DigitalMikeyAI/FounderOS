# 1. Purpose

This knowledge base provides FounderOS and Archie with structured
domain knowledge related to RV sales, customer discovery, product
selection, presentation, closing, delivery, follow-up, product
knowledge, dealership operations, and field development.

This document is not intended to function as a static training manual.

Its purpose is to establish the initial knowledge layer from which
FounderOS can:

- support real-world sales activity;
- reinforce training;
- assist with customer discovery;
- improve product-selection reasoning;
- support roleplay and practice;
- identify knowledge gaps;
- analyze future Field Reports;
- compare training with real-world experience;
- identify recurring patterns;
- generate coaching;
- and continuously improve the user's sales methodology.

The long-term objective is a feedback system:

TRAINING
→ APPLICATION
→ FIELD REPORT
→ ANALYSIS
→ LESSON
→ ADJUSTMENT
→ APPLICATION

### [PRODUCT]

### [FIELD]

### [QUESTION]

# 3. Archie Knowledge Behavior

When using this knowledge base, Archie should follow these rules.

1. Never invent missing product information.
2. Never present uncertain handwritten notes as verified facts.
3. Separate company training from personal methodology.
4. Separate observed field behavior from established policy.
5. Treat RV specifications as model-year and configuration dependent.
6. Recommend verification when towing, weight, payload, capacity,
   compatibility, safety, pricing, inventory, warranty, or other
   consequential information is uncertain.
7. Preserve useful disagreements between training, management guidance,
   and field observations.
8. Prefer learning from repeated evidence rather than isolated events.
9. Use Field Reports to refine coaching, not silently rewrite historical
   training material.
10. Clearly identify when Archie does not know something.

# 5. The Conversation Standard

## [PERSONAL] Five-Step Communication Loop

1. SLOW DOWN
2. ASK
3. SUMMARIZE
4. DON'T GUESS
5. ASK FOR THE NEXT STEP

This is a foundational communication pattern.

### Slow Down

Do not rush to demonstrate knowledge.

Allow the customer to explain their situation.

### Ask

Use purposeful questions.

Questions should uncover information necessary to understand:

- why the customer is shopping;
- how they intend to use the RV;
- who will travel with them;
- what they currently own;
- what they like;
- what they dislike;
- where they travel;
- what limitations exist;
- and what outcome they are trying to create.

### Summarize

Reflect the customer's situation back to them.

Example structure:

"So if I'm understanding you correctly..."

Summarization serves two purposes:

1. It verifies that the salesperson understood correctly.
2. It demonstrates to the customer that they were actually heard.

### Don't Guess

Never pretend to know something that is unknown.

If information is uncertain:

- acknowledge it;
- find the answer;
- verify it;
- return with accurate information.

Trust is more valuable than appearing knowledgeable.

### Ask for the Next Step

Every productive conversation should move toward an appropriate next
action.

The next step may be:

- gathering additional information;
- looking at another RV;
- comparing floorplans;
- checking towing information;
- completing a worksheet;
- scheduling another visit;
- following up;
- or moving toward purchase.

# 7. Relationship Before Information

## [TRAINING]

Build rapport before attempting to gather large amounts of information.

Be their friend first.

Then get information.

A customer should feel like they are having a conversation rather than
being interrogated.

## Practical Interpretation

Discovery should not feel like a questionnaire.

Rapport and discovery should flow together naturally.

Questions should emerge from genuine curiosity about the customer's
plans.

---

# 8. Customer-Centered Selling

## [TRAINING]

Ask customers about themselves first.

Learn what they see.

Sell what they see.

The salesperson may control the process, but the customer must remain
at the center of the conversation.

## [PERSONAL]

The goal is not to force the customer into the salesperson's preferred
RV.

The goal is to understand the customer's desired experience and help
identify a product capable of delivering it.

# 11. Step 2 — Meet & Greet

## [TRAINING]

The objective of the Meet & Greet is to establish rapport and begin
understanding the customer's reason for visiting.

Do not immediately overwhelm the customer with product information.

Do not begin by attempting to prove expertise.

Begin with the customer.

Potential objectives:

- establish comfort;
- introduce yourself;
- learn names;
- understand why they came in;
- determine whether they have visited previously;
- understand where they are in the buying process;
- and transition naturally into discovery.

Strong discovery makes the remainder of the process easier.

# 14. Discovery Before Product Selection

## [TRAINING]

Do not begin walking the lot randomly.

Product selection should follow discovery.

The salesperson should understand enough about the customer to make
an informed first selection.

## [PERSONAL]

Every unnecessary RV shown can increase confusion.

The objective is not:

"Let's look at everything."

The objective is:

"Based on what you've told me, I think we should start here."

# 15. Step 4 — Product Selection

Product selection connects customer needs to inventory.

Consider:

- RV category;
- towability;
- sleeping capacity;
- floorplan;
- bathroom configuration;
- kitchen configuration;
- storage;
- seating;
- outdoor features;
- travel style;
- campsite limitations;
- family size;
- comfort expectations;
- and budget when appropriate.

## [VERIFY]

Never assume an RV is towable solely because a customer owns a
particular model of truck.

Vehicle configuration matters.

Relevant variables can include:

- engine;
- drivetrain;
- axle ratio;
- cab;
- bed;
- payload;
- installed equipment;
- hitch;
- passenger/cargo load;
- and manufacturer limits.

Always verify consequential towing information.

# 16. Step 5 — Presentation

## [TRAINING]

A presentation should connect product features to customer needs.

Do not simply recite features.

Translate:

FEATURE
→ BENEFIT
→ CUSTOMER USE

Instead of:

"This RV has feature X."

Prefer:

"You mentioned X earlier. This feature could help because..."

## [PERSONAL]

A strong presentation proves that the salesperson listened during
discovery.

---

# 17. Feature → Benefit → Experience

Every major feature should answer:

"So what?"

Example structure:

FEATURE:
Large exterior storage compartment.

BENEFIT:
Provides additional storage capacity.

EXPERIENCE:
The family can carry outdoor equipment without filling the interior
living space.

The strongest presentations connect the RV to the customer's imagined
future experiences.

---

# 18. Step 6 — Trial Close

## [TRAINING]

The Trial Close tests whether the selected RV is moving toward the
customer's desired solution.

A trial close is not necessarily the final close.

Its purpose is to identify:

- enthusiasm;
- concerns;
- objections;
- missing requirements;
- uncertainty;
- or readiness to move forward.

## [PERSONAL]

The trial close should feel like checking alignment, not applying
pressure.

---

# 19. Step 7 — Worksheet

## [TRAINING]

The worksheet moves the interaction from product exploration toward
deal structure.

At this stage, accuracy and confidence matter.

## [PERSONAL]

"My job isn't to apologize for the payment."

Interpretation:

The salesperson should present information professionally rather than
prejudging the customer's financial decisions.

Do not create an objection for the customer before the customer has
expressed one.

---

# 20. Step 8 — Close

## [TRAINING]

Closing should follow sufficient discovery, product selection,
presentation, and trial closing.

The close should not compensate for weak earlier steps.

## [PERSONAL]

A strong close is often the natural result of a strong process.

When the customer has been understood, the product fits, concerns have
been addressed, and value is clear, asking for the business should not
feel disconnected from the conversation.

- tank capacities
- dimensions
- sleeping capacity
- appliance configuration
- generator equipment
- solar equipment
- towing compatibility

# 29. Weight Terminology

## [PRODUCT]

Important RV/towing concepts to master include:

### UVW

Unloaded Vehicle Weight.

The weight of the RV as defined by the manufacturer before certain
cargo/load additions.

### GVWR

Gross Vehicle Weight Rating.

Maximum permitted loaded weight of the RV as specified by the
manufacturer.

### CCC

Cargo Carrying Capacity.

Amount of allowable cargo capacity under the applicable weight
calculation.

### Hitch / Tongue / Pin Weight

The portion of trailer weight applied to the tow vehicle through the
connection point.

## [VERIFY]

Exact definitions and calculations should be confirmed against current
manufacturer/regulatory documentation when being used for an actual
customer towing decision.

# 33. Product Knowledge Learning Method

For each RV studied, learn:

1. WHO is this RV best for?
2. WHAT problem does the floorplan solve?
3. WHAT lifestyle does it support?
4. WHAT are its major differentiators?
5. WHAT should be verified before presenting it?
6. WHAT comparable units exist?
7. WHAT customer would probably dislike it?

This is more useful than memorizing specifications without context.

# 42. Archie Field Analysis

When sufficient Field Reports exist, Archie should look for patterns.

Examples:

"You are repeatedly reaching presentation but not worksheet."

"Customers frequently mention towing uncertainty during product
selection."

"Your strongest interactions occur when you spend more time in
discovery."

"You have encountered this objection four times this month."

"You repeatedly need help answering questions about electrical
systems."

"You have shown this floorplan several times but have not yet converted
one."

These observations should be evidence-based.

Archie should avoid drawing strong conclusions from tiny sample sizes.
